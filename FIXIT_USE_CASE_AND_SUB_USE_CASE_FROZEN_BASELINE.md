## Sub-Use-Case Baseline

The detailed sub-use cases already approved for the above functional areas are also frozen as part of this baseline. The approved coding convention is:

`UC-[AREA][MAIN NUMBER].[SUB NUMBER]`

Examples:
- UC-C31.1 Forgot Password
- UC-L02.1 Request Location Permission
- UC-S03.5 Activate Subscription
- UC-PR06.5 Activate Promotion
- UC-N07.4 Retry Delivery
- UC-F08.3 Quarantine Suspicious File
- UC-SYS08.4 Prevent Duplicate Write

The frozen sub-use-case scope includes the approved detailed decompositions for Customer, Provider, Company Personnel, Admin, Subscription, Promotions, Maps & Location, Communication, Media/File, Analytics/Monitoring, System/Audit, and Cross-System/Exception use cases.

## Frozen Cross-Cutting Business Rules

1. Provider `ACCEPT` means willing/available; it does not assign the provider.
2. Customer compares responding providers and makes the final provider selection.
3. Provider matching must obey exact approved FixIt service-area coverage, service, category, provider status, and availability rules.
4. Customer-to-provider service payment is off-platform; FixIt does not collect, hold, route, settle, or refund the service payment.
5. UC-C29 / UC-P20 / UC-P21 payment-related service records are optional and cannot block work start/completion solely because they are absent.
6. Provider-to-FixIt subscription and promotion charges are a separate platform billing flow.
7. Customer KYC is optional by default unless a separately approved higher-risk/legal/business rule requires it.
8. Multi-location requests create separate physical service requests for each service location while preserving group association.
9. Urgent requests do not bypass provider eligibility, geographic matching, or customer provider selection.
10. Exact customer address/location sharing follows the approved data-sharing and location-share rules.

## Change-Control Rule

Frozen does NOT mean immutable forever. It means this is the approved reference baseline.

Any future change must:
1. identify the affected use case/sub-use case;
2. identify affected business rules, database structures, APIs, UI, and tests;
3. be documented as a revision;
4. be reviewed/approved;
5. preserve the previous frozen baseline for traceability.

No use case or sub-use case should be silently deleted, renamed, merged, or semantically changed after this freeze.
